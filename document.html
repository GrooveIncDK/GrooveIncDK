What's actually inside an Omega Document.

Not a mood board. Not a logo file. A standards-grade specification, built the same way for every client — only the depth changes.

An Omega Document is not a mood board and not a logo file. It's a standards-grade specification — every section exists so that anyone, with no access to the original designer, could reproduce the brand exactly: the right ink, the right rotation, the right typeface at the right weight, on the right surface. The tier a client buys determines how many of these sections are built out, and to what depth — not whether the document exists at all.

THE EIGHT SECTIONS

Same order, every time.

The short version of everything above — what changes by tier is which sections are included and how deep each one goes.

1

Background & gradient treatment

The base visual field the brand sits on — for Groove Inc, a free-form gradient running from primary blue through primary gold to white. Specified as a named, repeatable recipe, not "whatever looked good."

2

Fonts

Four roles, named explicitly: Headlines (Groove Inc: FP Head Pro Italic Medium, 24pt), Body text (Exo Soft Light, 11pt), Web font — a separate, explicit substitution for screen use (Lato Regular, 12pt), and Intermediate headings/quotes (Raleway Extra Bold, 15pt, with a documented right-aligned variant).

3

Icon construction

The mark's build geometry — literal rotation angles and proportions (Groove Inc's icon: 15°, -25°, 6°, and a defined stroke width) — so the icon can be reconstructed without redrawing it from scratch or guessing at an existing export.

4

Logotype + icon = logo

How the wordmark and the icon combine into the single approved lockup — documented as an assembly, not just a flattened final file.

5

Sublines

The modular tagline slots the logo can carry depending on context — Groove Inc's logo pairs with either "Multimedia Lab" or "Different Goals — Different Solutions" depending on where it's used.

6

Primary colours

The one or two colours that carry the most brand weight, specified in every system needed to reproduce them: CMYK, RGB, Hex, and slots for RAL/PMS/NCS once confirmed. Groove Inc's primary blue (#0F4D5A) and primary gold (#8C7935) sit here.

7

Full colour system

The secondary and muted/tertiary palette. This is the section that scales most visibly by tier: a small brand might stop at primary + one secondary; a full system needs the whole palette specified before it's usable across print, web and physical material.

8

Backgrounds, usage & clear space

The rules for where the logo is and isn't allowed to sit — including a measured clear-space grid ("penalty area") defining the minimum air around the mark that no other element may enter. This is what stops a brand degrading the first time someone else touches the file.

Never a different document. Only a bigger or smaller one.

WHAT DELIBERATELY ISN'T IN THE BASE DOCUMENT

Two exclusions, stated as plainly as the inclusions.

Certification/badge systems never come bundled

Even at Environment tier, sustainability certification branding is a separate, YFP-attributed add-on, priced and scoped on its own. This keeps the core claim — "I specify your brand" — true regardless of whether a client wants or qualifies for the YFP layer.

Physical-material specification only appears at Environment tier

Essentials and Complete are print/screen documents. The jump to Environment is the jump from "your brand, correctly reproduced" to "your brand, correctly reproduced on your actual shopfront."
